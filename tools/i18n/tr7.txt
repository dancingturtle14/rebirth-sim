2395|How it works
2396|Era packs are weighted by each period's approximate regional population (population^0.6, so small eras still get a chance). Each year's death rate combines infant mortality, child mortality, adult background mortality and a Gompertz curve that rises with age, adjusted for class and health; historical events (war, famine, plague) add extra risk in the matching years.

2433|"Then & Now" compares era estimates with data for Hong Kong and the world today (Census and Statistics Department, UN IGME, UN WPP, UNESCO, FAO, UNHCR, WHO). "History cards" record how major events affected ordinary people, each with its source. The birth-vs-choices shares in "What this life taught me" come from the model: it compares 30 random births in the same era with 30 random lives from the same birth. It's a game model, not a social-science finding. Intergenerational status persistence in "Legacy" follows Clark, The Son Also Rises (2014). Your reflections are saved only on your own device.
